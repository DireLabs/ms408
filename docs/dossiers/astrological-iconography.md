## 1. The zodiac series: physical structure (f70v2–f73v)

- The zodiac occupies f70v2 through f73v: concentric rings of small, mostly female figures ("nymphs"),
  most holding a star, around a central emblem of the sign ([voynich.nu/illustr.html](https://voynich.nu/illustr.html)). **[C]**
- Sequence starts with Pisces (f70v2), not Aries — unusual for a freestanding zodiac but consistent with
  a March-first calendar convention. **[C]** (voynich.nu, illustr.html)
- Capricorn and Aquarius are missing; the gap coincides exactly with a lost folio (f74), so their absence
  is codicological, not a design statement. **[C]** (voynich.nu, illustr.html)
- Aries and Taurus each appear **twice** ("dark" and "light" variants), each with ~15 nymphs; the other
  signs carry ~30 nymphs each. **[C]** (voynich.nu, illustr.html) The reading of the doubled signs as
  half-sign (15°) divisions — implying the whole series encodes 30 units per sign — is an inference, not
  an observation. **[D]**
- Reported nymph counts are inconsistent across secondary sources (e.g., Pisces reported as 29 by some,
  30 by others; the ring-by-ring breakdowns differ between voynichportal.com and voynich.nu). **[C that
  the sources disagree; the counts themselves are ungraded until we count them → §9b.]**

## 2. The emblem cycle as a whole: best-match tradition

- The most systematic comparison in the literature is Marco Ponzi's pictorial-cycle analysis (with
  materials contributed by Darren Worley), reported as comparing ~131 European zodiac cycles on shared
  emblem features. Best-matching cycles originate from **Germany, France, Austria, Switzerland, and
  possibly Poland**, with dates ranging from ca. 1160 to ca. 1450; many individual motifs were already
  present in Central European cycles by the 12th century ([stephenbax.net/?p=1755](https://stephenbax.net/?p=1755)). **[C — single
  unrefereed study; methodology is feature-checklist similarity, not provenance-weighted.]**
- Zandbergen's synthesis: the VMS zodiac cycle is "most similar to others found in German manuscripts
  roughly between 1400 and 1500" ([voynich.nu/origin.html](https://voynich.nu/origin.html)). **[C]**
- Ewa Śnieżyńska-Stolot (Jagiellonian University, specialist in zodiac iconography) judged that the VMS
  signs "were redrawn from a calendar," with clothing details indicating mid-15th-century fashion; the
  Sagittarius cap with fox tail "points to Germany," though it also appears in Polish fashion
  (reported at [voynich.nu/illustr.html](https://voynich.nu/illustr.html)). **[C — expert opinion relayed by Zandbergen, no
  standalone publication located.]**
- Dieter Blume (author of the standard corpus on medieval astrological imagery) reportedly considered the
  VMS representations "quite idiosyncratic" and pointed to the Alfonso el Sabio manuscript tradition as
  relevant comparanda (voynich.nu/illustr.html). **[C — same relay caveat.]** The idiosyncrasy judgment
  matters: it cuts against any strong single-exemplar copying claim. **[D as interpretation]**

## 3. The crossbowman Sagittarius (f73v)

The strongest single localization datum in the whole illustration program, per the literature.

- VMS Sagittarius is a **human figure (not a centaur) holding a crossbow (not a hand bow)** — a doubly
  marked variant. Human-form Sagittarius is uncommon; the crossbow is rare. **[C]** (voynich.nu)
- **Sensfelder analysis:** in 2003 Jens Sensfelder, a German medieval-arms specialist, analyzed the drawn
  crossbow at Zandbergen's request; the analysis (translated by Zandbergen; also hosted via Pelling's
  ciphermysteries.com "Crossbow article") supports a 15th-century, plausibly German-area crossbow type.
  It was never formally published. **[C for existence and broad conclusion; the fine-grained typology is
  D — commentators note the drawing may be an archetype, not a portrait of a real weapon
  ([voynichthoughts.wordpress.com/crossbow-analysis/](https://voynichthoughts.wordpress.com/crossbow-analysis/)).]**
- **Dated manuscript parallels** (crossbow + human Sagittarius), found roughly a decade after the motif
  was first flagged (~2001), the first published by Zandbergen ca. 2009:
  - **St. Gallen, Cod. Sang. 827** — composite Latin computistic/astronomical volume (incl. Sacrobosco,
    *De sphaera*), written **1425–1428 in the Lake Constance region** (not at St. Gall itself); crossbow
    Sagittarius among planet-children/zodiac imagery. **[C]** (ciphermysteries.com 2017; stephenbax.net ?p=1656)
  - **Munich, BSB Cgm 7269** — German-language Book of Hours **written in Poland, dated 1463**; crossbow
    Sagittarius on the November calendar page; its Gemini is a couple in a bath. **[C]** (stephenbax.net
    ?p=1656; voynichportal.com)
  - Additional human-Sagittarius (with or without crossbow) comparanda cited across the Worley/Ponzi
    posts: Vienna ÖNB Cod. 3085 Han, Munich Cgm 312, Heidelberg Cod. Pal. Germ. 298, the Codex Schürstab
    (Zurich), St. Gallen Cod. Sang. 760, and the Estense *De Sphaera*. **[C — list assembled from
    stephenbax.net ?p=1656/?p=1755 and voynichportal.com; individual shelfmark details not all
    re-verified here → §9b.]**
- Consensus statement in this literature: the **crossbow variant is attested only in works from
  German-speaking areas** (including German cultural spheres in Poland/Bohemia), 15th century.
  **[C — an absence claim; only as strong as the survey coverage behind it.]**
- **Pelling's transmission hypothesis:** the crossbowman entered the zodiac series as a copying error
  within the *Iatromathematisches Hausbuch* family — a constellation-cycle crossbowman miscopied into the
  zodiac-sign roundel — making the VMS a downstream witness of that specific German family
  ([ciphermysteries.com 2017, Cod. Sang. 760 post](http://ciphermysteries.com/2017/07/26/cod-sang-760-iatromathematisches-hausbuch-voynich-hypothesis)). **[D — plausible mechanism, unproven stemma.]**

## 4. The month names: later addition, dialect debate

- Each zodiac medallion carries a month name in Latin script, **added after the manuscript was completed,
  in a different (non-Voynichese) hand**. Alignment: Pisces = March through Sagittarius = December — the
  month in which the sun occupies the sign, standard medieval calendar practice. **[C]** (voynich.nu;
  inamidst.com/voynich/months)
- Readings (Sauvaget's set, representative): *mars, aberil (×2), may (×2), jong/yony, iollet, augst,
  septe(m)b(r), octe(m)bre, nove(m)bre, decebre*
  ([voynichanalysis.wordpress.com 2012](https://voynichanalysis.wordpress.com/2012/03/25/about-month-names/)). **[C]**
- **Dialect debate — unresolved:**
  - *Occitan/Provençal:* the traditional identification (Pelling 2006 argued a Toulon-area Occitan;
    Panofsky in 1954 called the names "provincial French," having leaned Spanish/southern in 1932 —
    [voynichrevisionist.com 2019](https://voynichrevisionist.com/2019/01/19/military-cryptanalysts-panofskys-responses-of-1954/)). **[C for who claimed what; D for the identification.]**
  - *Northern French:* Sauvaget's documented counter-case — *jong*-type June spellings cluster in
    northern France/Flanders/Anglo-Norman England 1200–1500; a Picardy astrolabe (~14th c.) carries
    *may* and *octembre*; he places the writer "north of Paris but not necessarily in France"
    (voynichanalysis.wordpress.com; stephenbax.net ?p=1226). **[C for the comparanda; D for the localization.]**
  - Swiss/Alemannic contact forms (*aberil*, *augst*) have been raised as a third option, i.e., a
    French–German contact zone. **[D — blog-comment level.]**
- The honest summary in the literature: the names are Romance, consistent with ca. 1400 orthography, and
  **not securely assignable to one dialect region**; candidate zones (Occitania, Picardy/Flanders,
  Franco-German contact zones like Alsace/west Switzerland) span most of francophone Europe. **[C]**
- A frequently repeated suggestion that the month-name hand matches the f116v marginalia hand would, if
  true, tie the calendar labels to the manuscript's early ownership. Per **L12**, f116v is appendix-only:
  we catalog this and build nothing on it. **[D]**

## 5. The 30-nymph structure vs. known astrological schemata

- The arithmetic — 30 figures per sign, 15+15 for the doubled signs — matches **degrees per sign**
  (12 × 30 = 360). The literature therefore reads the series against per-degree astrology:
  *paranatellonta* / degree-image traditions transmitted into Latin Europe via Pietro d'Abano (13th–14th
  c.) and standardized in the *Astrolabium Planum* corpus (printed 1488, on earlier models), which assigns
  an image to every one of the 360 degrees ([voynich.nu/illustr.html](https://voynich.nu/illustr.html);
  heavenastrolabe.wordpress.com on the Angelus/Abano degree stars). Śnieżyńska-Stolot, the modern
  specialist on *Astrolabium Planum* iconography, is the expert most associated with reading the VMS
  series this way. **[C that the tradition exists and the comparison is made; D that the nymphs *are*
  degree personifications — VMS nymphs are near-uniform, whereas degree-image cycles are maximally
  varied, which is a real disanalogy.]**
- **Melothesia** (zodiac-man body-part correspondences) is the other stock zodiac schema in the
  iatromathematical *Hausbuch* genre, but it maps signs to body parts, not to 30 sub-units; nothing in
  the VMS rings resembles it structurally. Its relevance is contextual (it co-occurs in the candidate
  source genre), not structural. **[C/D]**
- If the nymph counts deviate from clean 30/15 (see §1), the degree reading weakens — which is exactly
  why the counts must be established by us, not quoted. **[D, testable → §9b]**

## 6. Named comparison group: Alsace / Lake Constance / southern Germany

- The two *dated* crossbow parallels bracket the Lake Constance region (Cod. Sang. 827, 1425–1428) and a
  German-cultural Polish product (Cgm 7269, 1463). **[C]**
- Pelling (2018) argued specific stock-figure links to the **Diebold Lauber workshop (Hagenau, Alsace)**:
  the VMS Gemini couple matching a Lauber marriage-scene exemplar recycled into a zodiac in a
  medical-astronomical hausbuch, and (with Koen Gheuens' observation) a distinctively misdrawn crayfish
  Cancer shared with a Lauber-illustrated *Buch der Natur*
  ([ciphermysteries.com 2018](https://ciphermysteries.com/2018/09/10/voynich-zodiac-gemini-and-the-workshop-of-diebold-lauber);
  [herculeaf.wordpress.com 2018](https://herculeaf.wordpress.com/2018/09/12/laubers-buch-der-natur-bull-and-crayfish/)). **[D —
  motif-match arguments from two authors, no stemma; Lauber workshop output is mostly 1420s–1460s.]**
- Worley's Heidelberg find (Cod. Pal. Germ. 298) and the wider Worley/Ponzi comparanda set concentrate in
  **southern Germany / Alemannic zone, ca. 1440–1470** (voynichportal.com "Zodiac Patterns – Human
  Sagittarius"; stephenbax.net series). **[C for where the comparanda sit]**
- Net localization picture from iconography alone: an **Alemannic/Upper-Rhine–Lake-Constance gravity**
  for the emblem models, with the month names adding a francophone hand early in the object's life —
  jointly compatible with a bilingual contact zone (Alsace, west Switzerland, Savoy fringe), but not
  proving production there: models travel, and the emblems could be copied at any distance from where the
  tradition originated. **[D — synthesis]**

## 7. Dating: iconography vs. the carbon envelope (1404–1438)

- Radiocarbon dating of the vellum: **1404–1438 (95% conf.)** (University of Arizona 2009; per
  voynich.nu and Wikipedia). **[C — published physical result, consumed not reproduced, per L2 scope]**
- The iconographic comparanda skew **later**: closest cycles cluster ca. 1440–1470 (Worley/Ponzi);
  Śnieżyńska-Stolot reads mid-15th-c. fashion; the dated crossbow twins are 1425–1428 and 1463. Only
  Cod. Sang. 827 sits inside the carbon window. **[C]**
- Three reconciliations circulate, all **[D]**: (i) vellum stored before use (mainstream view: years,
  not decades, for a whole consistent batch); (ii) VMS is an *early* witness of a motif family whose
  surviving comparanda are mostly later copies — survivorship bias in 15th-c. paper hausbuchs is real;
  (iii) the fashion/typology readings over-date drawings that are crude and conservative. The literature
  does not force a contradiction: 1425–1438 satisfies both the carbon window and the earliest dated
  parallel.
- Also noted for completeness: Toresella's stylistic reading of the *herbal* drawings as northern Italian
  ca. 1460 (voynich.nu/origin.html) conflicts with the carbon window in the same direction — the
  "iconography reads late" tension is not unique to the zodiac. **[C]**

## 8. (a) Constraints for W5/W6 — the envelope from astrological iconography alone

Treat these as the maximal defensible statements if *only* this dossier's evidence existed:

1. **Dating:** zodiac iconography is fully consistent with production **1420s–1430s** (intersection of
   carbon window and comparanda), and cannot by itself exclude 1440s–1460s. It provides no support for
   pre-1400 or post-1500 production. **[C]**
2. **Localization:** the emblem models point to the **German-speaking sphere, most specifically the
   Alemannic/Upper-Rhine/Lake-Constance zone**; the crossbow Sagittarius is the single sharpest datum and
   is unattested outside German-cultural areas per current surveys. **[C, absence-claim caveat]**
3. **A francophone layer is present early:** the month names were added by a Romance-writing (French or
   Occitan family) hand — so within the object's early life it passed through, or was made in, a
   milieu with French-dialect literacy. Combined with (2), a **bilingual contact region** is the most
   economical single-place reading, but two-place histories (made in German zone, labeled in French zone)
   are equally allowed. **[D]**
4. **Genre gravity:** the nearest relatives of the zodiac series are **calendar/computus and
   iatromathematical hausbuch material**, not learned Latin astronomy — consistent with the W4 working
   picture of a vernacular-practical rather than university milieu. **[D]**
5. **Anti-constraint (for W6 assumption audit):** experts closest to the material (Blume) call the cycle
   *idiosyncratic* — W5/W6 narratives should not assume a single identifiable exemplar was copied; and
   note the northern-Italy premise in L1/RESEARCH-PLAN §1 is in mild tension with this dossier's
   German-zone gravity. Both should be carried as rival localizations in W6b, not silently merged. **[D]**

## 9. (b) Flagged test candidates (for T1.2 schema / T2.x studies)

- **F-AI-1 (T1.2/T1.3):** per-ring **nymph and star counts** for every zodiac page, plus per-figure
  features (star held y/n, tailed star y/n, clothed/nude, headwear, barrel/tub y/n, label present y/n).
  Secondary sources disagree on the counts; the 30/15-degree hypothesis (§5) stands or falls on them.
- **F-AI-2 (T1.2):** emblem feature checklist matching Ponzi's comparison dimensions (Sagittarius:
  human/centaur, bow/crossbow, headwear; Pisces: paired fish + cord; Taurus/Aries: posture, feeding;
  Gemini: couple pose; Cancer: crayfish form; Scorpio: form class) — so our schema can reproduce and
  extend the 131-cycle comparison computationally rather than by eyeball (W3 full-coverage goal).
- **F-AI-3 (T2.6, under L20):** test whether star counts/configurations in the zodiac and astro sections
  encode any astronomically consistent structure against widely available period references
  (per-degree/paranatellonta lists, *Astrolabium Planum* degree images) — the locked L20 starting set;
  expand only on signal.
- **F-AI-4 (T2.3 anchor hunt):** the ~300 nymph labels are the largest label population in the MS; if the
  degree hypothesis holds, label ordering around rings is a candidate *sequence* anchor (degree numbers
  or star names) — a rare chance at structured, enumerable referents.
- **F-AI-5 (low priority):** re-verify the shelfmark list in §3 against library catalogs before any
  W5 claim cites a specific manuscript; assembled from blog posts, transcription errors are possible.
- **F-AI-6 (W6a):** the month-name hand ≟ f116v hand question — catalog only (L12), but record it in the
