## 2. The argument

Reconstructed from abstract + authors' summary [all C unless marked]:

1. **Frame.** The VMS features "herbal, pharmaceutical, astrological and anatomical illustrations,
   including hundreds of naked women" (abstract). The nude women — some "holding objects adjacent
   to, or oriented towards, their genitalia" — are, for the authors, the datum other content
   theories fail to explain; Brewer to media: given plants plus these figures, he "cannot fathom"
   a non-gynecological subject. [C — abstract; artnet 2024]
2. **A documented culture of concealment.** The paper assembles late-medieval evidence of
   self-censorship, erasure, page-excision and encipherment targeting gynecological, contraceptive
   and sexual material: they decode a 21-line cipher from northern Italy concealing an abortion
   recipe, and catalog removed leaves and deliberate erasures in medical MSS. [C — Conversation]
3. **Hartlieb as witness to motive.** Johannes Hartlieb (c. 1410–68), Bavarian court physician
   writing "about plants, women, magic, astronomy and baths," repeatedly voiced anxiety about
   circulating women's secrets in the vernacular, self-censored (avoiding post-partum care, female
   pleasure, coital positions, abortive plants in open texts), and recommended "secret letters"
   (ciphers) to hide abortifacient and contraceptive recipes. He is offered as evidence of
   *mentality and motive* — the authors do **not** claim he wrote the VMS. [C — abstract; Conversation]
4. **The Rosettes proposal (the paper's novel iconographic claim).** The nine-circle foldout
   ("Rosettes") "represents coitus and conception" [C — abstract]. Sub-claims [C — Conversation]:
   - the nine circles map onto the medieval **seven-chambered uterus** doctrine plus external
     genitalia (central circle, scalloped edge) and internal vaginal opening (top-left circle);
   - **five vein-like lines** from top-left toward center reflect al-Rāzī's teaching that five
     veins lie in the virgin vagina;
   - **yellow and blue fluids** = male and female "sperm" (two-seed theory), their mingling
     depicting conception, with motion implying female pleasure;
   - top-right / bottom-right circles = the **two uterine horns**;
   - the **castle** = possible wordplay on German *schloss* (castle; also female genitalia/pelvis);
   - **two suns** = Aristotelian solar heat nourishing the embryo.
5. **Authors' own caveats.** "Many features of the illustration are yet to be understood"; the
   reading is a proposal "worth close scrutiny," not proof; no translation of Voynichese is
   attempted or claimed. [C — Conversation]

**Program-relevant summary [D — our characterization]:** a *genre-and-motive* argument (why
someone would encipher such a book, and what one image might show), not a decipherment; compatible
with any W2 encoding-class outcome, but it makes checkable iconographic and milieu predictions (§8–9).

## 3. Johannes Hartlieb and his corpus

- **Life:** c. 1410 – 18 May 1468; Bavarian physician; served Louis VII of Bavaria-Ingolstadt and
  Albert VI of Austria in the 1430s, court physician to Duke Albert III of Bavaria-Munich from
  1440, then Sigismund of Bavaria from 1456. [C — Wikipedia, "Johannes Hartlieb"]
- **Kräuterbuch** (herbal, ~160 plants plus animal-derived drugs, vernacular Bavarian German,
  illustrated): dated c. 1440 by Wikipedia (ed. Speta 1980); other sources give c. 1462.
  **Date contested — flagged, not resolved.** [C existence; D date]
- **Secreta mulierum** (pseudo-Albertus Magnus) and **Trotula** German translations: 1450s per
  Wikipedia; other sources put the Trotula at 1460–65, dedicated to Duke Sigismund, "Trotula"
  elevated to a queen. Paired copies: UB Marburg Ms. 93; Heidelberg Cod. Pal. germ. 480/488.
  **Dates conflict — flagged.** [C translations/MSS; D dating]
- **The "secret letters" passage:** in his Secreta mulierum adaptation Hartlieb recommends ciphers
  for sensitive gynecological matter — Brewer & Lewis's linchpin quote. [C — via their
  abstract/Conversation; primary-text verification pending full paper access]
- **Buch aller verbotenen Künste** (1450s): anti-magic treatise; context for his censorious
  temperament. [C — Wikipedia]
- **Chronology tension (relevant to W6):** VMS parchment dates 1404–1438; Hartlieb's relevant
  writings are 1440s–60s, *after* the window. Brewer & Lewis use him as evidence of a *shared
  culture*, not authorship — but the reading still requires that culture to be active a generation
  before its best-documented witness. Critics pressed this (§6). [C dates; inference D]

## 4. The Trotula / secreta mulierum tradition, c. 1350–1450

- **Trotula ensemble:** three 12th-c. Salernitan texts (Conditions of Women; Treatments of Women;
  Women's Cosmetics) circulating as a compendium; the dominant later-medieval women's-medicine
  reference, ~130 extant Latin MSS plus vernacular translations. Standard edition: Monica H.
  Green, *The Trotula* (Penn Press, 2001). [C]
- **Genre shift "diseases → secrets":** from the 13th c., women's medicine was reframed as
  *secreta mulierum* — natural-philosophical "secrets" literature (generation, menstruation,
  embryology) written by and for men, with masculine appropriation and gatekeeping as through-line.
  Green, *Making Women's Medicine Masculine* (OUP 2008); "From 'Diseases of Women' to 'Secrets of
  Women'," *JMEMS* 30 (2000). [C]
- **Pseudo-Albertus Magnus, De secretis mulierum:** the genre's bestseller, with enormous
  late-medieval German circulation — the very text Hartlieb translated. [C]
- **Fit to the VMS window:** 1350–1450 is the peak of German/Italian vernacularization of this
  literature; anxieties about lay/female readers are documented in the genre's prologues. Brewer &
  Lewis's contribution is evidence those anxieties sometimes reached *encipherment*, not merely
  Latin-as-barrier or excision. [C]
- **Seven-chambered uterus doctrine:** a genuine medieval anatomical commonplace (pseudo-Galenic
  *De spermate* lineage; monograph: Reisert, *Der siebenkammerige Uterus*, 1986), abandoned after
  16th-c. dissection. Currency c. 1400–1450 is real; whether the Rosettes depicts it is the
  contested step. [C doctrine; mapping D]

## 5. Prior women's-medicine / balneological interpretations (the reading's pedigree)

- **Newbold 1928:** *The Cipher of Roger Bacon* (ed. Kent) already read Quire 13's drawings as
  biological/reproductive imagery (cells, gametes — allegedly via a Baconian microscope); the
  decipherment is discredited, but it is the earliest reproductive reading of Q13.
  [C — summarized at voynich.nu history-of-research pages]
- **"Balneological" as a section name:** the standard label for Q13 comes from repeatedly and
  independently noticed resemblance to *De balneis Puteolanis* (Peter of Eboli, 13th c.) bath-book
  illustrations — nymphs, pools, medicinal bathing [C — voynich.nu/illustr.html]; medicinal
  bathing and women's regimen overlap in period medicine, which is why Q13 has always attracted
  women's-health readings. [C/D]
- **Skeptical counter-tradition:** Pelling doubts literal balneology for Q13 ("Q13 and Voynich
  balneology sources...?", Cipher Mysteries, 18 Jun 2009; "Green Voynich pools? I really don't
  *think* so," 4 Mar 2011) and splits Q13 codicologically into Q13a "water/conceptual" vs Q13b
  "balneological". [C] JK Petersen reviewed the Balneis comparison ("The Baths of Puteolanis,"
  voynichportal.com, 13 Sept 2017). [C]
- **Gibbs 2017 (cautionary precedent):** Nicholas Gibbs, *Times Literary Supplement* (Sept 2017),
  claimed the VMS is a women's-health manual compiled from Trotula-type sources, with Voynichese
  as abbreviated Latin. Rapidly rejected: Lisa Fagin Davis showed the "translations" are
  ungrammatical Latin that "doesn't hold together". [C — Boing Boing 11 Sept 2017 roundup]
  **Program note [D]:** Gibbs shows the *subject* claim can outlive a failed *reading*; Brewer &
  Lewis deliberately decouple the two — their strength relative to Gibbs.
- **Community priors:** women's-medicine/gynecology proposals recur on the mailing lists and
  voynich.ninja long before 2024 (O'Donovan lists earlier proponents and complains B&L present
  the idea as newer than it is). [C — voynichrevisionist.com, 26 Apr 2024]

## 6. Critical reception of Brewer & Lewis

- **Peer review status:** published in a respected history-of-medicine journal (SHM, OUP) —
  unusual for Voynich content theories, and noted approvingly by commentators. [C]
- **Language Log (Victor Mair, 21 Apr 2024):** sympathetic ("one that to me makes a lot of
  sense"); commenters praised its limited, hedged claims; D. N. O'Donovan dissented at length.
  [C — languagelog.ldc.upenn.edu/nll/?p=63603]
- **D. N. O'Donovan (voynichrevisionist.com, April 2024, two posts):** the most extended public
  critique: (i) "we don't *know* where the manuscript was made" — the Bavarian/German placement is
  assumed; (ii) the radiocarbon window predates Hartlieb's medical career; (iii) prior
  women's-medicine proposals go unacknowledged; (iv) reading held objects as genital-directed is
  interpretation presented as observation; (v) weak codicological/art-historical grounding. [C]
  (Caveat [D]: O'Donovan is a polemical source with her own contested theory; weight accordingly.)
- **voynich.ninja thread 4240:** mixed. Support: period censorship of gynecology makes an
  enciphered presentation "at least possible" (bi3mw). Criticism: Antonio García Jiménez argued
  cosmological readings of the Rosettes remain more probable. Brewer presented at community
  "Voynich Day" 2024. [C — voynich.ninja/thread-4240]
- **Not found (searched 2026-07):** no dedicated Zandbergen (voynich.nu) written response; no
  dedicated Pelling/Cipher Mysteries post; no published scholarly review or SHM reply. Absence
  noted, not evidence of assent. [C]
- **Popular press** (artnet, IFLScience, ScienceAlert, ZME Science, etc., April 2024): wide,
  mostly uncritical amplification under "partly about sex" headlines; reception data only. [C]
- **Competing Rosettes readings the paper must displace [C/D]:** geographic/map readings (Pelling:
  Milan/Pavia/Venice), cosmological/T-O readings, architectural readings — all with prior
  standing; the coitus-conception reading enters an unusually crowded field for this one foldout.

## 7. Assessment for the program

- **Strongest, most portable finding [C]:** documented late-medieval *practice* of enciphering
  gynecological/contraceptive recipes (the decoded north-Italian abortion-recipe cipher; Hartlieb's
  explicit "secret letters" recommendation). Raises the prior that a c. 1420 enciphered
  medical-looking codex could conceal women's medicine — motive evidence independent of any VMS
  internal reading. Feeds W4's cipher-culture dossier too.
- **Weakest step [D]:** the Rosettes iconography — each sub-claim (7+2 chambers, five veins, two
  suns, schloss pun) is post-hoc pattern-matching against a rich doctrine set, with no independent
  anchor.
- **Untouched by the paper:** Voynichese itself. The theory constrains *content and milieu*, not
  encoding class; compatible with H2-style cipher or shorthand, and even (awkwardly) H3
  meaninglessness — though a meaningless text would gut the motive argument.
- **Milieu tension [D]:** the reading pulls toward a German/Bavarian orbit; our working premise
  says "likely northern Italian milieu." B&L's strongest cipher exhibit is northern Italian, so
  the tension is soft — but W5 must not let the Hartlieb frame silently Germanize the origin model.

## 8. Constraints for W5/W6 — what the reading predicts, checkable

If the VMS is (partly) an enciphered women's-medicine/secreta text, then:

1. **Genre structure:** Trotula/secreta texts are recipe-and-regimen collections → predicts
   recipe-like formal units (short repeating formulae, ingredient-list statistics, line-as-unit
   effects), especially in Q13 and recipes-section text. Testable against H4 once a recipe-genre
   control (ReF *Arzneibuch* prose, already in our German corpus) is in the benchmark.
2. **Topic geography:** W2's unsupervised topic induction should recover Q13 (and Rosettes text)
   as a coherent cluster with above-baseline lexical sharing with *specific* herbal/pharma folios
   (abortifacient/emmenagogue herbs recur), more than with astro folios. The rival
   balneological-only reading predicts Q13 coherence but no privileged herbal linkage.
3. **Rosettes affinity:** predicts Rosettes text is lexically closer to Q13 than to cosmological/
   astro pages; a map/cosmos reading predicts the opposite. Clean discriminating comparison our
   corpus metadata already supports.
4. **Language prior:** predicts German (Bavarian) or northern-Italian vernacular plaintext →
   weight ENHG in encoding-bracket experiments; a decisive Latin-only or Hebrew result would
   weaken the Hartlieb frame (not the general secrecy frame).
5. **Censorship signature:** the secrets-culture predicts physical suppression → check whether
   VMS missing folios concentrate in Q13/pharma sections vs. uniform loss (codicological tally
   from page metadata; consume published collation).
6. **Scribal stratification:** a women's-medicine booklet reading is cleaner if Q13 is scribally/
   dialectally homogeneous → check $H/$L homogeneity of Q13 and the Rosettes bifolio in ZL
   metadata (data exists; no number asserted here per L3).
7. **Timeline (W6 evidence board):** parchment 1404–38 vs. Hartlieb floruit 1440s–60s — the
   reading needs pre-1440 evidence of gynecological encipherment (the Italian cipher's date
   matters; verify in full text) or it dates the *culture* by its later witness.

## 9. Flagged statistical / annotation test candidates (for D5 schema + W3 anchor hunt)

Annotation features that would let the anchor hunt test B&L's specific claims (all flagged as
candidates, not commitments — D-items for the D5 schema decision):

- **Nymph-level features (Q13 + zodiac):** holds-object (y/n); object type; object orientation
  relative to body (toward abdomen/genitalia vs. raised vs. neutral); posture; abdomen rendering
  (distended y/n); headwear; in-pool vs. in-pipe. → Anchor test: do adjacent tokens/labels covary
  with "object-toward-genitalia" or with object type?
- **Fluid features (Q13, Rosettes):** color (green/blue/yellow), flow direction, mixing junctions.
  → Test B&L's yellow/blue two-seed claim: does page vocabulary stratify by fluid color?
- **Rosettes structural counts (cheap, decisive on the descriptive layer):** radiating lines from
  top-left rosette (B&L: five "veins"); sun emblems count/position (B&L: two); scalloped edge on
  central rosette; per-rosette label harvesting. → Verifies their *descriptions* on our scans
  before any interpretation is entertained.
- **Label-class behavior:** if Q13 labels name body parts/baths (closed nomenclature), label
  type/token statistics should differ from star- and plant-label sections (smaller inventory,
  higher cross-page repetition). Comparable now via locus-type metadata.
- **Cross-section token linkage:** targeted co-occurrence scan Q13 ↔ herbal ↔ pharma for shared
  stems (per §8.2) — an emmenagogue-herb lexicon effect would be the reading's best possible
  statistical anchor; its absence is a citable constraint.
- **Non-testable claims to quarantine [D]:** schloss wordplay; al-Rāzī five-veins attribution;
  Aristotelian sun symbolism — no annotation-level or statistical handle; grade D regardless of
  annotation effort.
