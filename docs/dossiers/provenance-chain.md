## 1. The documented chain, forward order (overview)

Unknown origin (parchment 1404–1438) → **[gap ≥150 yrs]** → (?) Rudolf II, Prague (≤1612) →
Jacobus Horčický de Tepenec (ex libris, 1608–1622) → (?) → Georgius Baresch, Prague (by 1637) →
Johannes Marcus Marci (by 1662, on Baresch's death) → Athanasius Kircher, Rome (1665/66) →
Collegium Romanum library → hidden as private property of Fr. General Petrus Beckx (post-1870,
Villa Torlonia, Castel Gandolfo) → Wilfrid Voynich (1911/12) → Ethel L. Voynich (1930) →
H. P. Kraus (1961, $24,500) → gift to Yale, Beinecke MS 408 (1969). [C — composite; per-link grades below]

## 2. The links, backward from 1912, each graded

### Link 1 — Voynich (1912) ← Jesuits. **Grade C (strong)**
- Voynich acquired the MS in 1911/12 from the Jesuits via Fr. Joseph Strickland "upon promise of
  absolute secrecy"; he invented a discovery story ("chest in a castle in Southern Europe") and
  maintained it until his death (1930). His widow's sealed letter (opened after her death, 1960)
  disclosed the true source. [C — voynich.nu/history.html]
- The MS was among manuscripts removed from the Collegium Romanum after the Italian state's
  confiscation of Jesuit property (1873); books were relabelled "Ex Bibliotheca privata P. Petri
  Beckx" (the Jesuit General) to shield them as personal property. [C — voynich.nu/history.html]
- **Correction to the popular account:** archival work by Francesca Potenza shows the cache was
  hidden at **Villa Torlonia, Castel Gandolfo**, not Villa Mondragone (Frascati); the Mondragone
  story traces to Kraus (1962) and ELV's letter ("Frascati") and "should no longer appear in modern
  literature." Also: the Jesuit manuscript sale to the Vatican was *planned* from 1903 but executed
  **June 1912**. [C — voynich.nu/extra/checklist.html]
- Post-1912: ELV inherits (1930) → Kraus buys (1961, $24,500) → donated to Beinecke, Yale (1969). [C]

### Link 2 — Jesuits/Collegium Romanum ← Kircher. **Grade C (strong)**
- Kircher (1602–1680) was a Collegium Romanum professor; his library and museum passed into the
  College's collections. The MS survived the 1773 suppression of the Society and the 1824–1870
  period (rebinding after woodworm damage to wooden boards is documented). [C — voynich.nu/history.html]
- Corroboration that the MS physically reached Rome: Kinner to Kircher, 5 Jan 1667 (APUG 562,
  f.151) notes it was "brought to Rome by the father provincial of Bohemia" (1665). [C — voynich.nu/letters.html]

### Link 3 — Kircher ← Marci (1665 letter). **Grade C (the anchor link)**
- Marci to Kircher, **19 August 1665** (long cited as "1665 or 1666"; Zandbergen: "The correct
  reading is 1665"), found *with* the manuscript by Voynich in 1912; now Beinecke **MS 408A**.
  Marci, nearly blind, dictated it; he donates the book and reports its Prague history.
  [C — voynich.nu/letters.html; voynich.nu/extra/checklist.html]
- Independent corroboration in the Kircher carteggio (Archivio della Pontificia Università
  Gregoriana, APUG): Kinner to Kircher, 4 Jan 1666 (APUG 562, f.138) asks whether Kircher has made
  progress on "the mysterious book which Marci had sent." [C — voynich.nu/letters.html]
- Note: the MS 408A letter is the *only* document in the chain that travels with the object itself;
  everything upstream of it is testimony inside it or in the APUG carteggio. [C]

### Link 4 — Marci ← Baresch. **Grade C (good, slightly inferential)**
- Marci 1665: the book was "bequeathed to me by an intimate friend"; Baresch is unnamed there, but
  Marci to Kircher, 12 Sep 1640 (APUG 557, f.127r) explicitly recommends "M. Georgius Barschius"
  and forwards his material, tying the two men and the book together. Marci inherited Baresch's
  library (Baresch d. 1662). [C — voynich.nu/letters.html; voynich.nu/history.html]

### Link 5 — Baresch ownership (by 1637). **Grade C (strong)**
- Baresch to Kircher, **27 April 1639** (APUG 557, f.353; located in the carteggio in 1998):
  describes the MS ("From the pictures of herbs… stars and other things bearing the appearance of
  chemical symbolism… my guess is that the whole thing is medical"), and references an earlier 1637
  transmission of transcribed samples via Th. Moretus (that first letter is lost). [C — voynich.nu/letters.html]
- Independent corroboration: Kircher to Moretus, **12 March 1639** (Czech National Library VI B 12b,
  f.73; found by Josef Smolka, publ. 2010) acknowledges the mysterious script and rules out
  Glagolitic for it. [C — voynich.nu/letters.html]
- Baresch "was essentially unknown to Czech historians until recently" — his obscurity is itself a
  point against a fabricated chain (no one would invent him). [C — voynich.nu/history.html; D for the inference]

### Link 6 — Baresch ← Tepenec. **Grade D (undocumented; pure gap-bridge)**
- No document connects them. Zandbergen's reconstruction has Baresch obtaining it after Horčický's
  death (1622), date and mechanism uncertain; "inherited" is conventional shorthand, not evidence.
  This is the weakest link *inside* the documented Prague period. [D — voynich.nu/history.html]

### Link 7 — Tepenec ownership (1608–1622). **Grade C (physical evidence)**
- Erased/faded inscription "**Jacobj à Tepenece**" in the bottom margin of **f1r**, legible under
  UV; most scholars read it as an **ex libris** rather than an autograph. [C —
  [voynich.nu/history.html](http://www.voynich.nu/history.html);
  [Wikipedia: Voynich manuscript](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voynich_manuscript)]
- Jakub Horčický z Tepence (Jacobus Sinapius, c. 1575–1622): imperial distiller/chemist and
  director of Rudolf II's botanical gardens; ennobled "de Tepenec" 1607/08 (sources vary — flagged
  §8). The inscription therefore brackets possession to **1608×1622**, the earliest hard *terminus
  ante quem* for the MS's existence before radiocarbon dating. [C]
- **Ink/erasure evidence:** the name (and a nearby number) faded or was erased — by whom and when
  is unknown. Voynich (and later Brumbaugh) applied chemicals to develop it; Beinecke's Voynich
  papers include a rotograph annotated "before it was chemically restored," so a pre-treatment
  photographic record exists. The chemical treatment episode is a long-running controversy
  (accusations that Voynich enhanced or even planted it are countered by the pre-treatment photos
  and by the signature matching Horčický's documented hand — the match claim needs verification,
  flagged §8). [C for treatment history — [Cipher Mysteries](https://ciphermysteries.com/2009/01/07/the-tepenecz-f1r-signature),
  [Wikibooks f1r](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/The_Voynich_Manuscript/F1r); D for authenticity disputes]
- Related f1r trace: a faded/erased marginal **column key** (alphabet sequences), imaged
  multispectrally in 2014, images released 2025 — read as a 17th-c. decryption attempt, with a
  recent attribution to Marci's circle. [D — recent, unverified; flagged §8]

### Link 8 — Tepenec ← Rudolf II. **Grade D (plausible, undocumented)**
- No document. Speculative mechanisms: imperial gift, or seizure/settlement after Rudolf's death
  (1612), when the emperor "still owed Horčický… a significant amount of money." Zandbergen: "We
  don't know how or when Horčický obtained the Voynich MS." [D — voynich.nu/history.html]

### Link 9 — Rudolf II purchase (600 ducats, Mnishovsky). **Grade D (secondhand hearsay, twice removed)**
- Sole source: Marci's 1665 letter. "Dr. Raphael, tutor in the Bohemian language to Ferdinand III…
  told me the said book had belonged to the Emperor Rudolph and that he presented the bearer who
  brought him the book 600 ducats." Raphael Sobiehrd-**Mnishovsky** (1580–1644) died two decades
  before the letter; Marci reports a conversation from memory, and Zandbergen stresses the
  "told me" and questions whether the price covered the MS alone or a lot of books.
  [C for what the letter says; **D for the fact asserted** — voynich.nu/history.html]
- Same passage carries the **Roger Bacon attribution**: Mnishovsky's *belief* (not knowledge) that
  the author was Roger Bacon; Marci himself suspends judgment on it in the letter. This single
  hearsay clause is the entire origin of the "Bacon cipher MS" tradition (§4). [C/D as above]

### Link 10 — Seller to Rudolf II: the Widemann candidate. **Grade D (archivally grounded hypothesis)**
- Stefan Guzy (HistoCrypt/Voynich Conf. 2022) searched ~6,900 imperial Hofkammer journal entries,
  finding 126 book transactions in 1576–1612. The **only** match to a high-value mystery-book sale:
  **March 1599**, a "couple of remarkable books" (alchemical MSS) bought from Augsburg physician
  **Carl Widemann** for **600 florins**, intermediary Zacharias Geizkofler; recorded in Austrian
  state archives and the Geizkofler family archive (Ludwigsburg). Guzy: this "seems to present a
  way" the MS entered the collection — explicitly not proof; no entry names the Voynich MS.
  [C for the archival record; **D for the identification** —
  [Guzy 2022, CEUR-WS Vol-3313 paper 16](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3313/paper16.pdf)]
- Currency mismatch: Marci says 600 **ducats**; the 1599 record says 600 **florins/gulden** — same
  number, different (lower-value) unit. Consistent with garbled oral transmission, but a real
  discrepancy. [C — Guzy 2022; voynich.nu/extra/checklist.html]
- Guzy's negative results matter too: no matching item in the Hofbibliothek inventories (Blotius
  1597, Tengnagel 1609/19) or the Kunstkammer inventory (Fröschl 1606–11); if Rudolf owned it, the
  Kunstkammer is the likeliest (uncatalogued) home. [C — Guzy 2022]
- Upstream speculation: Widemann's friendship with botanist/Orient traveller **Leonhard Rauwolf**
  as a possible earlier trail. [D — Guzy 2022, offered as conjecture]

## 3. The Dee/Kelley hypothesis — origin and modern standing

- **Origin:** Wilfrid Voynich himself (1921 lecture/paper, "A Preliminary Sketch of the History of
  the Roger Bacon Cipher Manuscript"), needing a carrier from England (Bacon) to Prague (Rudolf):
  John Dee, who was in Bohemia 1584–1589 with Edward Kelley and owned Roger Bacon manuscripts. [C]
- Supporting scraps, all weak: Dee's diary notes possession of 630 ducats (Oct 1586); Sir Thomas
  Browne's report of Arthur Dee recalling his father with "a booke… containing nothing but
  Hieroglyphicks" in Prague. Neither identifies the Voynich MS; the Arthur Dee book is generally
  identified with other candidates (e.g., the Book of Soyga). [C for the sources; D for any link]
- **Modern standing: rejected.** Zandbergen: the Dee sale story "is based entirely on a hypothesis
  of W. Voynich… no longer accepted nowadays"; checklist: "Rudolf II did not buy the manuscript
  from John Dee" — the attribution "should no longer appear in modern literature." Guzy 2022:
  Voynich's Dee thesis "was lacking any firm evidence and soon was dismissed." Kelley-as-author
  variants (hoax hypotheses) persist in popular literature but have no documentary support.
  [C — voynich.nu/history.html; voynich.nu/extra/checklist.html; Guzy 2022]

## 4. The "Bacon" attribution — origin

- Entire basis: the Mnishovsky hearsay inside Marci's 1665 letter (Link 9). Voynich amplified it
  into the MS's working title for decades and back-fitted the Dee trail to it (§3). [C]
- Killed by physical evidence: radiocarbon dating of the parchment (U. Arizona, 2009) to
  **1404–1438** (95%) — Bacon died 1292. Note the checklist's caveat: the *ink* was never
  forensically dated; the argument is "parchment would typically be used shortly after it was
  prepared." [C — voynich.nu/extra/checklist.html; Hodgins in Clemens (ed.) 2016]

## 5. The pre-1600 gap — documented 15th–16th-c. traces

Between parchment production (1404–1438) and Prague (≤1599/1608) there is **no documented owner**.
Zandbergen: "We have no further evidence about the earliest history of the MS." [C] Traces:
- **Zodiac month names** (marginal, non-Voynichese) in a Romance dialect — candidates include
  NW French/Occitan; read by Zandbergen as possible early French-sphere ownership. [C for the
  marginalia; D for localization — voynich.nu/history.html]
- **f116v marginalia** ("michiton oladabas" line) mixing pseudo-Voynichese with German words
  ("pox leber…"), in a hand judged 15th-c. — German-speaking milieu contact early in its life. [C/D]
- **15th-c.-style quire numbers** (Latin ordinal abbreviations), suggesting early binding order was
  imposed in a Germanic abbreviation tradition. [C/D]
- Iconographic dress-dating (e.g., the Sagittarius crossbowman's costume, c. 1420–1450, German
  sphere) — consistent with radiocarbon but not provenance evidence proper. [D]
- Candidate trails into the gap, all D: Widemann←Rauwolf (§2, Link 10); Dee/Kelley (§3, rejected).

Summary: the chain is **C-solid from Baresch (1637) to the present**, **D everywhere upstream of
Baresch except the Tepenec ex libris (C)**, and empty before ~1599.

## 7. Constraints for W6b narratives

Any origin narrative must route through ALL of the following or explain them away:
1. Parchment made 1404–1438; no dated physical evidence of the *writing* itself (ink undated). [C]
2. The object is physically in **Prague by 1608×1622** (Tepenec ex libris) — every narrative needs
   a path from creation locale to Rudolfine Prague. [C]
3. The Rudolf II purchase is **hearsay only**; narratives may use it but must not treat the 600
   ducats, the imperial ownership, or the Bacon attribution as fixed points. [C about the evidence]
4. The Baresch→present segment is essentially closed; hoax/late-forgery narratives must beat the
   carteggio's independent corroboration (Kircher–Moretus 1639, found only in 2010 — a successful
   out-of-sample prediction of the traditional chain). [C]
5. Dee/Kelley is available only as an explicitly rejected-by-specialists trail; using it costs a
   narrative its plausibility budget. [C for the rejection]
6. Pre-1600 marginalia constraints: early contact with a German-speaking milieu and Romance-dialect
   month names must be jointly accommodated. [C/D]
7. Negative space: no owner's mark, colophon, or catalogue entry for ~180 years despite an alleged
   imperial-level price — narratives should address why the object left so few traces. [D framing]
