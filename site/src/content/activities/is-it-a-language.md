## The big idea

The Voynich Manuscript is a 600-year-old book that **nobody can read**. So how do
researchers study it? They look for *patterns* — the fingerprints that real writing
leaves behind — without needing to know what any word means. In this activity, learners
become "repetition detectives" and do the same thing.

## Set-up

Give each group three short samples, unlabeled (A, B, C):
- **A** — a paragraph of ordinary English.
- **B** — random gibberish (mash the keyboard, or scramble letters).
- **C** — a short passage of Voynich transliteration (e.g. `daiin daiin qokedy qokedy chol chol shedy`).

Don't tell them which is which yet.

## Steps

1. **Hunt for repeats (10 min).** In each sample, highlight any word that appears more
   than once. Which sample repeats words the most? Which the least?
2. **Measure word length (5 min).** Are the words mostly short, mostly long, or a mix?
   Real languages usually have lots of short common words (*the, a, of*) and fewer long ones.
3. **Look at word beginnings/endings (5 min).** Do many words start or end the same way?
   (The Voynich text does — lots of words look like cousins of each other.)
4. **Make your call (5 min).** Which sample do you think is a real language? Which is
   nonsense? Which is the mystery? **Rate your confidence: A, B, C, or D** (see below).
5. **Reveal & discuss (10 min).** The surprise: the mystery text (C) has *strong* patterns
   — it repeats and has word-families like a language — **but that still doesn't prove it
   means anything.** Nonsense can be patterned too.

## Confidence grades (borrowed from the real research)

Researchers on this project grade every claim:
**A** = very sure (checked many ways) · **B** = fairly sure · **C** = a guess with some
evidence · **D** = just a hunch. Ask learners to grade their own guesses — and to say
*why*.

## The takeaway

You can learn a lot about writing by counting patterns, even in a language nobody
understands. But a pattern is a **clue, not a conclusion** — the honest answer about the
Voynich Manuscript is still "we don't know."

## Extend it

Older learners can try the real tool: an adult can run each sample through
`python -m ms408` and compare the "verdict" to the class's guesses.
