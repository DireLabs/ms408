## The big idea

Before anyone counts a single statistic, research starts with **looking carefully**. The
whole Voynich Manuscript is digitized in stunning detail and free to explore. Learners
become observers of a real 15th-century mystery object.

## Set-up

Open the Beinecke Library's digitized MS 408 (search "Beinecke MS 408 digital"). Pick a few
pages: a plant ("herbal") page, a circular "astronomical" page, and a page of dense text.

## Steps

1. **See, don't guess yet (10 min).** On the worksheet, list only what you can *see*:
   colors, shapes, how many drawings, whether text wraps around pictures. No opinions yet.
2. **Now think (10 min).** For each observation, write what you *think* it might mean.
   ("The plant doesn't match any real plant I know" → "maybe it's imaginary?")
3. **Grade your confidence (10 min).** For each idea, mark **A/B/C/D**:
   A = very sure, D = just a hunch. Which of your ideas are actually A's? (Usually very few!)
4. **Compare groups (10 min).** Did different groups "see" different things on the same
   page? How does that happen? (This is why researchers cross-check each other — and why
   this project has an independent reviewer try to *disprove* every claim.)

## The takeaway

Good research separates **what you see** from **what you think it means**, and is honest
about how sure it is. Most first ideas are C's and D's — and that's fine. Being careful
about confidence is what makes it science.
